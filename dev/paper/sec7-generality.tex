\section{Beyond joint models}\label{sec:generality}

Nothing in Sections~\ref{sec:absorbable}--\ref{sec:rotation} depends on
the survival submodel except Condition~\ref{cond:S}. To check that the
gains are not specific to joint models, we applied the construction, in a
standalone NumPyro program that does not use \texttt{jmjax}, to two
classical mixed models, each with a subject-constant covariate:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{Orthodont} \citep{potthoff1964,pinheiro2000}: a Gaussian
linear mixed model for the distance measure of 27 children at 4 ages,
$\text{distance} \sim \text{age} \times \text{male} + (1 + \text{age} \mid
\text{child})$ with age centred. Sex is subject-constant, so both
random-effect columns have absorbable directions $\{\mathbf{1},
\mathrm{male}\}$, $k = 2$.
\item The toenail onychomycosis trial \citep{debacker1998,lesaffre2001}: a
logistic mixed model for 1908 binary outcomes on 294 patients, $y \sim
\text{trt} \times \text{time} + (1 \mid \text{patient})$. Treatment is
subject-constant, so the absorbable directions are $\{\mathbf{1},
\mathrm{trt}\}$.
\end{itemize}
Both use the non-centred parameterization with $\N(0, 10^2)$ priors on
$\beta$, half-normal(5) priors on the standard deviations and an LKJ(2)
prior on the correlation, which is the parameterization brms and rstanarm
use by default. Three arms were run on each, with 4 chains of 1000 warm-up
and 1000 sampling iterations and 3 seeds: unrotated with a diagonal metric,
rotated with a diagonal metric, and rotated with the dense $(\beta, U)$
block. Because each run takes only a few seconds including compilation, we
report ESS per 1000 gradient evaluations, which does not depend on the
machine.

Table~\ref{tab:gen} shows the same pattern as the joint model. The
intercept and the subject-constant coefficients gained 5.5--7.1 times per
gradient, and the coefficients absorbed by the random slope in
\texttt{Orthodont} 2.4--2.5 times. The rotation with a diagonal metric
alone supplied a factor of 2.3--2.8 on the intercept and subject-constant
coefficients and nothing on the slope-absorbed ones; the dense block
supplied the rest, as Proposition~\ref{prop:ridge} predicts.
Per second, including compilation, the gains on those coefficients were
4.1--5.1. Posterior means agreed with the unrotated fit's to within 0.15
posterior standard deviations, $\hat R \leq 1.011$ everywhere, and there
were no divergences.

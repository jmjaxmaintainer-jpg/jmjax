\begin{abstract}
\noindent
In a regression model with subject-level random effects, the likelihood
identifies the sum of a subject-constant fixed effect and the matching
combination of random effects, but not how that sum is split between them.
Under the non-centred parameterization that Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC)
software uses by default, only the prior pins the split, and the result is
a narrow ridge that slows the sampler on exactly the coefficients an
analyst reports. We characterize the random-effect directions that fixed
effects can absorb, for any design, give a construction that does not
depend on how the design matrix happens to be parameterized, and state the
condition a survival submodel adds in joint longitudinal-survival models.
One fixed orthogonal rotation of the standardized random effects, the same
for every random-effect column, turns these directions into a small block
of sampling coordinates. It is exact for every value of the random-effect
covariance: prior, posterior and every reported quantity are unchanged. A
dense mass-matrix block over this block and the fixed effects then removes
the ridge. In simulation the rotation raised the effective sample size per
second of the intercept and the time slope by factors of 14 and 21
(geometric means); on two standard joint-model datasets the intercept and
subject-constant covariates gained 17--28 times; and the same coefficients
gained 5--7 times per gradient evaluation in a linear and a logistic mixed
model outside the joint-model setting. A second finding concerns the association parameter of a joint
model. For an ideal two-block sampler that draws it exactly from its
conditional distribution given the other parameters, the lag-1
autocorrelation equals the fraction of its posterior variance those
parameters explain. On real data that fraction is at least 0.85--0.87,
mostly shared with the baseline hazard, which caps such a sampler at one
effective draw in 13--15 and accounts for
about three quarters of the measured gap between HMC and a
Metropolis-within-Gibbs implementation.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Keywords:} Hamiltonian Monte Carlo; non-centred parameterization;
identifiability; preconditioning; mixed models; joint models.
\end{abstract}

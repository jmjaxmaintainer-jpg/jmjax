\section{Real data}\label{sec:realdata}

\subsection{Rotated against unrotated}

We fitted two standard joint-model datasets, both distributed with
JMbayes2, twice from the same preliminary mixed-model and Cox fits and the
same sampler seed, unrotated
and rotated, with 4 chains of 1000 warm-up and 1000 sampling iterations
and 3 seeds per dataset.
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{aids} \citep{abrams1994,guo2004}: 467 patients,
$\sqrt{\mathrm{CD4}} \sim \mathrm{obstime} + \mathrm{drug} +
\mathrm{gender}$ with a random intercept and slope on \texttt{obstime}, and
a hazard for death depending on drug. There are three absorbable intercept
directions (constant, drug, gender) and one slope direction.
\item \texttt{pbc2} \citep{murtaugh1994}: 312 patients, $\log(\mathrm{serum\
bilirubin}) \sim \mathrm{year} + \mathrm{age}$ with a random intercept and
slope on year, and a hazard for death depending on drug. There are two
absorbable intercept directions (constant, age) and one slope direction.
\end{itemize}

The intercept and subject-constant coefficients gained 17--28 times, and the time slope, also on an absorbable direction, 2.3--4.4 times (Table~\ref{tab:real}). Every posterior mean agreed
between the two fits within Monte Carlo error (largest paired difference
1.65 Monte Carlo standard errors over 19 parameter-dataset pairs). The two
datasets differ in one instructive way. On \texttt{aids} the unrotated
trajectories were three times longer: the degeneracy was what set NUTS's
trajectory length, and removing it made every iteration cheaper, so every
parameter gained, $\alpha$ included, and sampling time fell by 40\%. On
\texttt{pbc2} the trajectory length was set by something else, and the
parameters the degeneracy does not involve changed by 0.82--1.28 times ($\alpha$ 0.95).

With a random intercept only, the same datasets show the degeneracy more
starkly. Unrotated, $\beta_0$ and the subject-constant coefficients mixed
at 0.04--0.07 ESS per draw, and 91--95\% of their posterior variance was
explained by the absorbable directions of the random effects; every other
parameter mixed near one ESS per draw. The rotation raised the affected
coefficients' ESS per second 51--53 times on \texttt{aids} and 19--20
times on \texttt{pbc2} (2 seeds each), and the slowest parameter's ESS per
draw from 0.05 to 0.88 and from 0.06 to 0.78.

\subsection{A blocked sampler}

JMbayes2 \citep{jmbayes2} fits the same models by Metropolis-within-Gibbs
and hierarchically centers the coefficients it can, which is the classical
remedy for the location degeneracy \citep{gelfand1995}. It therefore
offers two comparisons: on the centered coefficients, of the rotation with
centering; on the rest, and on $\alpha$ in particular, of HMC with a blocked
sampler (Section~\ref{sec:blocked}). JMbayes2 was run with 4 chains of
16,000 iterations and 4,000 burn-in, well above its defaults, and its time
is the whole model-fitting call. To keep the ESS estimator from driving the
comparison, we saved the draws of all three fits and computed every ESS in
Table~\ref{tab:jmb} with the same estimator, NumPyro's. The ratios moved
little: with the rank-normalized bulk ESS of the \texttt{posterior}
package applied to all fits they were within 5\% of those in the table,
and with each package's own estimator within 18\% (JMbayes2's own
estimator is somewhat more generous to it). These fits are a separate run
from those of Table~\ref{tab:real}, with the same settings.

The unrotated NUTS fit trailed JMbayes2 by a factor of 17--33 on the
coefficients JMbayes2 centers (Table~\ref{tab:jmb}). Rotated, it is at
parity on them (0.88--0.98) and ahead on the others: 1.9--7.7 times on
the time slope, 1.8--2.8 on $\gamma$ and 7.2--7.9 on $\alpha$. The rotated NUTS fit therefore achieves efficiency comparable to JMbayes2 on the regression coefficients that JMbayes2 centers, while retaining the non-centered parameterization for the random-effect variances. The lead on $\alpha$ is present without the rotation, and Section~\ref{sec:blocked} attributes most of its per-draw part to blocked updating; the rotation added to it on \texttt{aids} (3.5 to 7.2) by shortening NUTS's trajectories. JMbayes2 produces 4.6--7.3 times as many draws per second, but only one effective draw in 31--40 for $\alpha$ (Figure~\ref{fig:alpha}).
